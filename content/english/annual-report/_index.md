# 2026 Highlights ⚡

## Figures
- **Call for papers:** 47 proposals (2025: 49), 30 accepted (32), 2 invited talks (i.e., 6.6%; 2025: 4 i.e., 12.5%), acceptance rate: 64% (65%)
- **Audience:** 170 registered people, 128 checked-in, no-show rate: 24.7% (2025: 170 registered, 140 checked-in, 17.6% no-show)
  <br>+ between **45 and 70 live stream users** throughout the event! 💚
- **Budget:** ~€14,400 (2025: €13,500) with the following distribution: 48% catering (47%), 30% speaker reimbursements (24%), 15% goodies (16%), 7% servers, banking, etc. (10%).

These **figures are relatively stable, and we are quite happy with them**:
- A sufficient number of submissions allowed us to build a high-quality agenda of talks and workshops.
- An audience of 130–170 people is now a well-established level for mixing good research sharing, valuable interaction, and friendly moments.
- A level of sponsorship that allows us to provide the community with an event featuring expert speakers and quality social moments.

## Improvements

**Some improvements and modifications have been identified** by the team and/or in the speakers’ and audience feedback forms:
- **The last afternoon did not have any break.** It featured 3 long (35-minute) talks and 2 short (20-minute) ones with no breaks in between. It was a gamble, and it didn’t pay off 👉️ **We will not do this again.**
- **A structural lack of speaker diversity** (gender and background, among other factors). As in almost every year (with 2025 perhaps as an exception), we faced this issue, as have many other conferences in the field 👉️ **We need to try harder and differently!**
- **Wi-Fi availability:** We were not sure until shortly before the event started whether we would be able to provide Wi-Fi access, or under what conditions. Some attendees were ultimately unable to use it 👉️ **Next year, we will assess its availability much earlier and will clearly communicate the conditions during the registration process.**
- **Pronoun usage:** Several attendees asked us to provide a way to indicate their pronouns. We will look into how to properly implement pronoun usage, from collecting them from people who wish to specify them to displaying them as clearly and legibly as possible on badges.
- **Workshop handling:** We received feedback asking for more consistent workshop handling (and you are right!) 👉️ For each workshop submission, we will ask for a precise list of requirements and the maximum number of attendees the speaker wants in the room.

**When specific concerns are raised** through the feedback forms by identified attendees or speakers, we provide them with a direct answer. We share two of them here because we believe they may be of general interest:

- **AI**
  *“I was quite disappointed to learn that the stickers had been created using AI. I’m the first to use AI, but I think it would be much nicer to commission artists for this sort of thing [...]”*
  <br>👉️ **Our (much shorter) answer:** *We have produced our own designs for years. We tried for several years to work with a particular artist, but it failed mainly due to their availability. Finally, AI enabled us to make the most of the limited time each volunteer could devote to organizing the event, while quickly coming up with two or three designs during one of the planning meetings that suited us all.*

- **Managing questions after talks**
  *“I don’t really understand the point, as a speaker, of waiting until you’ve picked up a microphone at the end of the talk to ask if there are any questions, rather than simply letting us ask for questions ourselves. I’d find that more natural, and that’s what I see at other conferences, but I’m sure you have a very good reason for doing it this way.”*
  <br>👉️ **Our answer:** *“We retake control, as we are responsible for managing the conference schedule in relation to the audience, the current speaker, and the speakers to follow. It is up to us to decide whether there is time for questions—or not. It is also up to us to ensure speakers rotate smoothly so that no one monopolizes the microphone. All of this is part of the organizers’ work and responsibility, and the slight dip in the flow is not a big deal, in our humble opinion, given the benefits it brings to the organization.”*
