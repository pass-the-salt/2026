2026 Conference report

Pass the SALT is a free access, English-spoken, 3-day conference dedicated to Free Software and Security. It features a single track of talks with some parallel workshops.

This document is our annual report for the 2026 edition of the conference. We share all the figures, insights, and analysis about the event.

2026 Highlights ⚡

This section is the TL;DR of this report to give you main news, facts and feedbacks from this 2026 edition.

Figures

  • Call for papers: 47 proposals (2025: 49), 30 accepted (32), 2 invited talks ie 6,6% (4 ie 12,5%), acceptation rate: 64% (65%)
  • Audience: 170 registered people, 128 checked-in, no-show 24.7% (2025: 170, 140, 17.6%)
    + between 45 and 70 live stream users all along the event! 💚
  • Budget: ~14.400€ (2025: 13.500€) with following dispatch: 48% Catering (47%), 30% speakers reimbursement (24%), 15% goodies (16%), 7% servers, bank etc (10%).

These figures are relatively stable and we are quite happy with them:

  • A sufficient number of submissions allowed us to build an agenda of high quality talks and workshops.
  • The audience of 130-170 people is a now well established level to mix good research sharing, valuable interaction and friendly moments.
  • A level of sponsoring that allows us to provide to the community an event with expert speakers and quality social moments.

This means a great deal to us!

Improvements

Some improvements/modifications have been identified by the team and/or in the speakers and audience feedback forms:

  • The last afternoon has not any break. And it was filled with 3 long (35min) talks and 2 shorts (20min) ones without any break. It was a gamble, and it didn’t pay off 👉️ We won’t do it again.
  • Structural lack of speakers diversity (gender and background among other factors). As almost each year (maybe 2025 as an exception), we faced it as many other conferences of the field 👉️ We have to try harder and differently!
  • Wifi availability. We weren’t sure until near the start of the event that we would be able to provide wifi access and under which conditions. Some of the attendees finally weren’t able to use it 👉️ next year, we will assess much earlier its availability and will expose during the registration process very clearly under which conditions it will be.
  • Use of pronouns. Several feedback asked us to provide a way to indicate their pronouns. We will be looking into how to implement the use of pronouns properly, from collecting them from people who wish to specify them to displaying them as clearly and legibly as possible on badges.
  • Workshops handling. We got feedback asking for a more consistent wqorkshops handling (you are right!) 👉️ we will ask for each workshop submission a precise list of asked requirements and the maximum number of attendees the speaker wants to have in the room.

When precise concerns are raised through the feedback forms by identified attendees or speakers, we provide them directly our answer. We share here two of them because we think they may have a general interest:

  • AI “I was quite disappointed to learn that the stickers had been created using AI. I’m the first to use AI, but I think it would be much nicer to commission artists for this sort of thing […]”
    👉️ Our (far shorter here) answer: we have produced for years our own designs. We have tried during several years to work with a particular artist but it failed due mainly to agenda availability on his side. Finally, AI enabled us to make the most of the limited time each volunteer could devote to organising the event, whilst quickly coming up with two or three designs during one of the planning meetings that suited us all.

  • Managing questions after talks "‘I don’t really understand the point, as a speaker, of waiting until you’ve picked up a microphone at the end of the talk to ask if there are any questions, rather than simply letting us ask for questions ourselves. I’d find that more natural, and that’s what I see at other conferences, but I’m sure you have a very good reason for doing it this way."
    👉️ Our answer: “We take control again, as we are responsible for managing the conference schedule in relation to the audience, the current speaker and the speakers to follow. It’s up to us to decide whether there’s time for questions… or not. It’s also up to us to ensure the speakers rotate smoothly so that no one monopolises the microphone. All of this is part of the organisers’ work and responsibility, and the slight dip in the flow isn’t a big deal, in our humble opinion, given the benefits it brings to the organisation.”.
